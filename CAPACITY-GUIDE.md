# Capacity and Well-being Management - User Guide

## Overview

The Capacity and Well-being Management feature allows students to share their current responsibilities and availability with their project team. This promotes open communication, mutual understanding, and realistic task distribution among team members.

## Purpose

By sharing your capacity information:
- Team members understand your workload and commitments
- Task assignments become more fair and realistic
- Reduces stress from overcommitment
- Fosters empathy and supportive team dynamics
- Helps prevent burnout and promotes well-being
- Enables better project planning and timeline estimation

## How to Set Your Capacity

1. Navigate to your project
2. Click the **Capacity** tab
3. Click **Set My Capacity** (or **Update My Capacity** if you've already set it)
4. Fill out the capacity form (all fields are optional)
5. Click **Save Capacity Information**

## Information You Can Share

### Internship / Part-time Job
Share details about any work commitments:
- Company or organization name
- Role or position
- Hours per week
- Schedule (if relevant)

**Example:**
```
Software Engineering Intern at ABC Tech (20 hours/week, Mon-Fri afternoons)
```

### Organizations & Affiliations
List any student organizations, clubs, or extracurricular activities:
- Organization name
- Your role or position
- Time commitment

**Example:**
```
- Student Council Secretary (5 hours/week)
- Computer Science Society Member
- Debate Team Captain (8 hours/week)
```

### Other School Responsibilities
Mention other academic commitments:
- Number of courses you're taking
- Teaching assistant roles
- Other major projects
- Exam periods

**Example:**
```
Taking 6 other courses this semester, TA for Database Management (10 hours/week), Major capstone project for another class
```

### Personal Responsibilities
Share personal matters that affect your availability (optional):
- Family commitments
- Health considerations
- Caregiving responsibilities
- Long commute times

**Example:**
```
Taking care of elderly parent on weekends, medical appointments every Tuesday morning
```

### Estimated Hours Available Per Week
Indicate how many hours you can realistically dedicate to this project:
- Helps team plan task assignments
- Enables realistic timeline estimation
- Prevents overcommitment

**Example:**
```
10 hours/week
```

### Additional Notes
Any other information about your schedule or availability:
- Busy periods (midterms, finals)
- Upcoming travel
- Preferred working hours
- Best days for meetings

**Example:**
```
Available for meetings on Wednesdays and Fridays. Will have reduced availability during finals week (Nov 20-27)
```

## Viewing Team Capacity

All project members can view everyone's capacity information:

1. Go to the **Capacity** tab in your project
2. See all team members who have shared their capacity
3. Review each member's:
   - Current commitments
   - Available hours per week
   - Additional notes
   - Last update date

## Best Practices

✅ **DO:**
- Be honest about your actual availability
- Update your capacity when circumstances change
- Share enough detail for the team to understand
- Use this to have open conversations about workload
- Respect others' capacity constraints
- Consider capacity when assigning tasks

❌ **DON'T:**
- Overcommit to appear more available
- Judge others for their commitments
- Share overly personal or sensitive information
- Ignore capacity information when distributing tasks
- Use this information against team members

## Privacy and Visibility

**Important Notes:**
- All capacity information is visible to all project members
- Only share what you're comfortable with the team knowing
- You can update or remove information at any time
- This is meant to foster understanding, not judgment

## Example Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Heavy Course Load

**Situation:** You're taking 7 courses this semester and have limited time.

**What to Share:**
- **Other School Work:** "Taking 7 courses (21 units) including 3 lab courses"
- **Availability:** "5-8 hours/week"
- **Notes:** "Very busy Tuesdays/Thursdays with back-to-back classes"

**Benefit:** Team understands why you can't take on too many tasks.

---

### Scenario 2: Working Student

**Situation:** You have a part-time job to support yourself through school.

**What to Share:**
- **Internship/Job:** "Barista at Coffee Shop (25 hours/week, mostly evenings and weekends)"
- **Availability:** "10 hours/week for project"
- **Notes:** "Free mornings and Monday/Wednesday afternoons"

**Benefit:** Team can schedule meetings when you're available and assign tasks accordingly.

---

### Scenario 3: Active in Student Organizations

**Situation:** You're heavily involved in campus activities.

**What to Share:**
- **Organizations:** "VP of Engineering Society (15 hrs/week), Volunteer at Community Center (4 hrs/week)"
- **Availability:** "12 hours/week"
- **Notes:** "Extra busy during event planning weeks (once per month)"

**Benefit:** Team understands your fluctuating availability.

---

### Scenario 4: Family Commitments

**Situation:** You have caregiving responsibilities at home.

**What to Share:**
- **Personal:** "Family caregiving responsibilities on weekends"
- **Availability:** "15 hours/week (weekdays only)"
- **Notes:** "Available for weekday meetings, cannot commit to weekend work"

**Benefit:** Team won't schedule weekend activities and understands your constraints.

---

### Scenario 5: Health Considerations

**Situation:** You have medical appointments that affect your schedule.

**What to Share:**
- **Personal:** "Regular medical appointments"
- **Availability:** "12 hours/week"
- **Notes:** "Thursday mornings unavailable, may need occasional flexibility"

**Benefit:** Team is aware without needing specific medical details.

## When to Update Your Capacity

Update your capacity information when:
- You join a new project
- Your circumstances change (new job, dropped a class, etc.)
- Your availability changes significantly
- You have an upcoming busy period
- Your commitments end or reduce

## Using Capacity for Task Assignment

When assigning tasks, consider:

1. **Available Hours:** Don't assign 20 hours of work to someone with 5 hours available
2. **Existing Commitments:** Someone with heavy course load may need longer deadlines
3. **Schedule Constraints:** Assign urgent tasks to those with flexible schedules
4. **Fairness:** Balance workload based on actual capacity, not just task count
5. **Team Support:** Help overloaded members by redistributing tasks

## Integration with Other Features

### With Tasks
- Check member capacity before assigning tasks
- Consider availability when setting deadlines
- Adjust workload based on capacity constraints

### With Disputes
- Reference capacity when raising uneven distribution disputes
- Use capacity data to support workload analysis
- Helps resolve conflicts about task assignments

### With Contribution Tracking
- Understand that lower contribution might reflect limited capacity
- Evaluate contribution relative to available time
- Recognize quality over quantity

## Benefits of Transparency

Sharing your capacity helps create:
- **Realistic expectations** among team members
- **Fair task distribution** based on actual availability
- **Reduced stress** from overcommitment
- **Better communication** about workload
- **Supportive team culture** that respects individual circumstances
- **Higher quality work** when tasks match capacity

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is this information required?**
A: No, all fields are optional. Share only what you're comfortable with.

**Q: Can I see who viewed my capacity?**
A: No, but all project members can view all capacity information.

**Q: What if my situation is temporary?**
A: Use the "Notes" field to indicate temporary situations and update when things change.

**Q: Should I share medical details?**
A: No. Only share what you're comfortable with. "Health considerations" or "Medical appointments" is sufficient.

**Q: What if I have more capacity than others?**
A: That's okay! Capacity varies. The goal is honest communication, not comparison.

**Q: Can capacity information be used against me?**
A: This is meant to foster understanding, not judgment. Report any misuse to your professor.

- Support **well-being** and prevent burnout
- Create a **supportive** team environment

Your well-being matters. Be honest about your capacity, and respect others' capacity constraints.
